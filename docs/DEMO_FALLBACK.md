## Approved intervention from the verified run

**Teacher-approved summary:** Students incorrectly believe that sunlight is the actual food that plants consume, rather than an energy source used to create food. Teacher-verified.

**Learning objective:** Students will distinguish energy sources from matter and identify that plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose.

**Corrected concept:** Plants produce glucose by using light energy to rearrange carbon dioxide and water. Sunlight is an energy source, not a food source.

**Suggested activity:** Use a “Recipe for Photosynthesis” card. Label sunlight as the energy source and carbon dioxide plus water as the ingredients. Ask students why a plant would stop growing if it had sunlight but no water or carbon dioxide.

**Analogy:** Sunlight is like heat from an oven: the heat is needed to bake a cake but is not the cake. Carbon dioxide and water are the ingredients that become glucose.

**Follow-up checks:**

1. Is sunlight matter that becomes part of the plant’s mass? Expected: no; it supplies energy.
2. What ingredients do plants use to make glucose? Expected: carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil.
